## 1. Primary audiences and their needs

Full detail already exists in `BUSINESS_PRIORITIES_AND_AUDIENCES.md`
(ten profiles). For brand-strategy purposes, four matter most because
they carry the highest trust burden and the clearest need for a
positioning to speak to directly:

| Audience | Core need the brand must answer |
|---|---|
| **Residential vendors** | "Will this agent get me a fair price without inflating the valuation to win my instruction?" |
| **Landlords (residential & commercial)** | "Will my property, and my money, actually be protected and properly managed?" |
| **Buyers & tenants** | "Can I trust what I'm seeing is real and current, and will someone actually get back to me?" |
| **Existing managed tenants** | "If something goes wrong, will it get fixed without a fight?" |

Every audience profile shares one underlying anxiety this brand can
credibly answer, because it's actually true of how the site is built:
**a fear of being oversold to** — an inflated valuation, a padded fee,
a listing that isn't really available, an AI pretending to be a
person. Positioning around straightforwardness is not just a tone
choice; it is defensible against what's actually been built (see §2).

## 2. Brand positioning and differentiators

**Positioning statement (working, provisional):**

> Times Estate Ltd is the London estate agency for people who want a
> straight answer before a sales pitch — proper advice from a real
> person, clearly stated regulatory standing, and nothing promised
> online that can't be delivered in person.

**Differentiators, each supported by a confirmed fact or a verifiable
operational truth (not a marketing claim invented for effect):**

1. **No inflated online valuations.** The site is built so it
   structurally never states a valuation figure — every "valuation"
   routes to a human-arranged, in-person appraisal (`valuation-terms.ts`,
   `AI_SAFETY_RULES.md`). Most portals and several competitors reviewed
   in `MARKET_RESEARCH.md` lead with an instant, automated estimate.
2. **Transparent about its own regulatory status, not just its
   claims.** Every credential (redress scheme, Client Money
   Protection) is shown with its real verification status, not
   asserted as fact until it clears an independent check
   (`COMPLIANCE_REGISTER.md`) — an unusual level of self-disclosure for
   an estate agency website.
3. **An AI assistant that says what it can't do.** The site's AI
   assistant is built to refuse legal, financial, and negotiation
   questions outright and never claims to be human — a deliberate,
   tested safety contract (`AI_SAFETY_RULES.md`), not a generic
   chatbot disclaimer.
4. **Registered, real company, not a trading name of convenience.**
   Companies House number 09597081, incorporated 2015 — a decade-old,
   verifiable legal entity (`CONTENT_REGISTER.md`).
5. **Repair reporting that doesn't disappear into a portal login.**
   A prominent, simple repair-reporting route for managed tenants,
   identified in `MARKET_RESEARCH.md` as an under-used differentiator
   among competitors reviewed.

**What this positioning deliberately avoids claiming:** years of
experience, number of properties sold/managed, awards, or client
testimonials — none of these are confirmed, and `CONTENT_REGISTER.md`
explicitly prohibits inventing them. The positioning is built entirely
from what can be honestly said today.

## 3. Brand personality and tone of voice

**Personality, as three adjective pairs (each paired with its
necessary opposite, to avoid drifting into a cliché):**

- **Straightforward, not blunt** — direct language, but warm enough
  that "no" or "not yet confirmed" doesn't read as dismissive.
- **Established, not old-fashioned** — confidence from being a real,
  registered, decade-old company, expressed in modern, plain language
  rather than formal or archaic phrasing.
- **Knowledgeable, not showy** — expertise demonstrated by clarity and
  precision (exact terms, real numbers, honest caveats), never by
  jargon or unearned superlatives ("award-winning," "leading").

**Tone of voice — do and don't:**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "A member of our team will contact you to arrange a visit." | "Get an instant valuation in seconds." |
| "This does not confirm a viewing." | Implying a form submission is a booking. |
| "Requires verification" / "Missing" shown honestly | Silence where a fact should be, or a vague "coming soon" |
| Plain contractions ("we'll," "you're") | Corporate stock phrases ("we pride ourselves on") |
| Active voice, short sentences | Passive voice, superlatives, exclamation marks |

## 4. Messaging hierarchy

1. **Core promise (always present, rarely stated outright):**
   Straight answers, properly handled, from people you can actually
   reach.
2. **Three supporting pillars:**
   - **Honest advice** — no inflated figures, no automated promises.
   - **Properly protected** — clear, checkable regulatory standing.
   - **Actually responsive** — a real repair-reporting route, a real
     phone number, a human at the end of every enquiry.
3. **Proof points (used only where genuinely true today):** structurally
   never states a valuation figure; every regulatory credential shown
   with its real status; a landline and mobile number now published;
   AI assistant with a tested, published safety contract.
